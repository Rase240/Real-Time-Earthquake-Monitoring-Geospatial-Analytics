Limitations:

* Counts aggregate a whole 10-degree cell; the forecast says nothing about where in the cell, when in the week, or how large any individual earthquake will be.
* Most of the forecast skill comes from persistence of ongoing sequences (recent counts); the model cannot anticipate a sequence that has not started.
* Intervals assume a negative-binomial distribution whose dispersion depends only on the predicted mean (not on the region); coverage is checked on the test period and varies by region.
* Counts are integers, so an interval cannot hold exactly 80% or 95% probability; compare empirical coverage with the model-implied coverage, which is at least the nominal level.
* Catalogue completeness at M4.5 varies by region and over time; counts are compared only within each cell, never across cells.
* Live features use the USGS feed at the forecast origin; events posted late lower the most recent count.

Methods: `poisson_plugin`: Poisson quantiles around the fitted expected count (parameters treated as exact). `parameter_uncertainty`: Parametric simulation: 4000 draws of (log K, [log c], p) from the MLE's normal approximation (inverse Hessian), Poisson count per draw; nothing is tuned on the evaluated sequences. `parameter_uncertainty_nb`: As parameter_uncertainty, but the count per draw is negative binomial with Var = lam + d * lam^2; d is fitted by maximum likelihood (mixture over the parameter draws) on mainshocks before 2013 only.

Limitations:

* M4.5+ global catalogue: only the largest aftershocks are seen, and early aftershocks are missing while the mainshock coda masks them.
* Radius from rupture-length scaling is a heuristic; nearby independent activity is included.
* Sequences with a larger later event were excluded, which favours simple sequences.
* This evaluation is offline; the application never extrapolates fits beyond the data.
